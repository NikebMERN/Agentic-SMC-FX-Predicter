1. How SMC/ICT traders think from high timeframe to entry

The core idea is:

Higher timeframe = direction
Middle timeframe = setup/story
Lower timeframe = entry confirmation

Many ICT resources describe the main concepts as liquidity, fair value gaps/imbalances, displacement, market structure shift, BOS, CHOCH, order blocks, daily bias, premium/discount, OTE, and killzones.

Recommended AI timeframe breakdown
| Timeframe      | Purpose              | What the AI should look for                                                                |
| -------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Daily / 4H** | Main bias            | Major trend, key highs/lows, previous day/week high/low, premium/discount, major liquidity |
| **2H / 1H**    | Market structure     | BOS, CHOCH, trend continuation, trend reversal, clean displacement                         |
| **1H / 30M**   | Setup zone           | FVG, order block, liquidity sweep, support/resistance liquidity zones                      |
| **15M / 5M**   | Confirmation         | MSS, retracement into FVG/OB, rejection candle, clean invalidation                         |
| **3M / 1M**    | Optional scalp entry | Only for tight ICT scalping models; more noise, so lower confidence                        |

Your example is close, but I would improve it like this:

4H: find the main dealing range, liquidity, premium/discount, major support/resistance.
1H: identify trend, BOS/CHOCH, and whether price is moving toward buy-side or sell-side liquidity.
30M/15M: find FVG, order block, liquidity sweep, and displacement.
5M/3M/1M: wait for entry confirmation, not just “deadline.” The “deadline” is usually a session window or killzone, not a reason to enter by itself.

ICT’s Silver Bullet model, for example, is described as a time-based setup using liquidity and FVGs, with common one-hour windows around London open, New York AM, and New York PM in New York local time. The same source describes using the 15-minute chart for liquidity/bias and 1-minute or 3-minute charts for execution.

2. Buy assumption logic

A strong BUY_BIAS should not come from one sign only. It should come from a story like this:

4H/Daily bias: price is bullish or price is in discount inside a larger range.
Liquidity event: price sweeps sell-side liquidity below a previous low.
Rejection: price closes back above the swept low.
Structure shift: on 1H/30M/15M, price creates bullish CHOCH or MSS.
Displacement: bullish candle moves strongly upward and creates an FVG.
Entry zone: price retraces into bullish FVG or bullish order block.
Invalidation: stop/invalidation is below the swept low or order block low.
Target: next buy-side liquidity, equal highs, previous high, or previous day high.

The AI output should be:

BUY_BIAS — confidence 68% — invalid if price closes below X — target liquidity near Y

Not:

“Buy now, guaranteed.”

3. Sell assumption logic

A strong SELL_BIAS is the opposite:

4H/Daily bias: price is bearish or price is in premium inside a larger range.
Liquidity event: price sweeps buy-side liquidity above previous high/equal highs.
Rejection: price closes back below the swept high.
Structure shift: on 1H/30M/15M, price creates bearish CHOCH or MSS.
Displacement: bearish candle moves strongly downward and creates an FVG.
Entry zone: price retraces into bearish FVG or bearish order block.
Invalidation: above swept high or order block high.
Target: sell-side liquidity, equal lows, previous low, or previous day low.

The AI output should be:

SELL_BIAS — confidence 71% — invalid if price closes above X — target liquidity near Y

4. When the AI should say NO TRADE

This is very important. A good trading AI must refuse bad setups.

Return NO_TRADE when:
| Problem                             | Why it matters              |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| 4H says bullish but 1H says bearish | Timeframes conflict         |
| No liquidity sweep                  | No clear smart-money story  |
| FVG is too small                    | Could just be spread/noise  |
| No candle close confirmation        | Wick-only breaks are weak   |
| Price is in middle of range         | Bad risk/reward             |
| Spread is high                      | Entry may be unreliable     |
| No invalidation level               | Risk cannot be defined      |
| Major news period                   | Technical patterns may fail |
| Confidence below threshold          | Model is not sure enough    |

5. Best structure for your SMC/ICT AI

Your AI should work like this:

User selects pair
↓
Fetch OANDA candles
↓
Analyze Daily/4H bias
↓
Analyze 1H structure
↓
Analyze 30M/15M setup
↓
Analyze 5M confirmation
↓
Score SMC signals
↓
Score ICT signals
↓
Apply risk filters
↓
Return BUY_BIAS / SELL_BIAS / WAIT / NO_TRADE
↓
After prediction horizon, verify actual market movement using OANDA
↓
Compare AI prediction vs real price movement
↓
Compare user feedback vs market result
↓
Store training record for admin review

OANDA candle data can support this because its instrument/candlestick model includes candle time, bid/ask/mid candle data, volume, completion status, and OHLC price data.

What “threshold” means?

 - A threshold is the minimum required amount before the AI accepts something as valid.

=> Simple meaning:

    “Do not count this as a real signal unless it passes this minimum level.”

Examples:
| Threshold                       | Meaning                                                                    |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `minBosBreakPips = 2`           | A BOS is valid only if price closes at least 2 pips beyond the swing level |
| `minFvgSizePips = 1.5`          | Ignore FVGs smaller than 1.5 pips because they may be noise                |
| `maxSpreadPips = 2`             | If spread is above 2 pips, do not enter or reduce confidence               |
| `minConfidenceForBias = 0.60`   | AI must be at least 60% confident before giving BUY/SELL bias              |
| `minRiskReward = 2.0`           | Potential reward must be at least 2 times the risk                         |
| `equalHighLowTolerancePips = 3` | Two highs can be considered “equal highs” if they are within 3 pips        |
